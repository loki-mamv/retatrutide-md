Does Retatrutide Carry a Thyroid Cancer Warning?

Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Not yet, because retatrutide has no FDA label. Lilly said on September 29, 2026 that it plans to file for approval in the first quarter of 2027. Approved drugs in the same class, such as Zepbound, carry a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rats. Retatrutide's Phase 3 trials excluded people at known risk.

That answer has two parts. One is a regulatory fact: no label means no boxed warning. The other is a scientific question: does this kind of drug raise thyroid cancer risk in people? This page covers both. For the wider safety picture, see our retatrutide side effects guide.

Does retatrutide have a boxed warning today?

No. A boxed warning is part of an FDA-approved prescribing label. Retatrutide is still investigational, so it has no label of any kind.

Lilly's July 23, 2026 release said the company is completing the manufacturing data package for its Biologics License Application and "plans to subsequently submit retatrutide in Q1 2027" (Lilly, July 2026). On September 29, 2026, Lilly repeated that it "plans to submit a Biologics License Application (BLA) in Q1 2027" (Lilly, September 2026). As of late November 2026, we found no report that Lilly has filed. Our FDA approval timeline explains what happens after a filing.

The FDA decides label wording during review. So nobody can say today what retatrutide's label will contain. What we can say is what its approved relatives already carry.

What does the warning on approved GLP-1 drugs say?

Zepbound (tirzepatide) is Lilly's approved dual agonist. Its label opens with a boxed warning titled "Risk of thyroid C-cell tumors" (Zepbound label, DailyMed). Here is what it says, point by point:

  • The finding: "In rats, tirzepatide causes dose-dependent and treatment-duration-dependent thyroid C-cell tumors at clinically relevant exposures."
  • The uncertainty: It is "unknown" whether the drug causes these tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), in humans.
  • Who cannot use it: People with a personal or family history of MTC, and people with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
  • What to tell patients: Doctors should explain the possible MTC risk and the symptoms of thyroid tumors.
  • Screening: Routine calcitonin blood tests or thyroid ultrasound are "of uncertain value" for early detection.

MTC is a rare thyroid cancer that starts in C-cells. These cells make the hormone calcitonin. Most thyroid cancers start in other thyroid cells. MEN 2 is an inherited syndrome that carries a high lifetime risk of MTC.

What did the rat studies actually find?

The concern comes from two-year rodent studies, not from human cases. The Zepbound label gives the numbers for tirzepatide:

  • Rats received 0.15, 0.50, or 1.5 mg/kg twice weekly for 2 years. That equals 0.1 to 1 times the human exposure at the 15 mg weekly dose.
  • C-cell adenomas (benign tumors) rose significantly in male rats at 0.5 mg/kg and above, and in female rats at every dose.
  • Adenomas and carcinomas combined rose significantly in both sexes at every dose.
  • A 6-month study in transgenic mice found no tumors at doses up to 10 mg/kg.

The label states that the "human relevance" of the rat tumors "has not been determined" (Zepbound label). Retatrutide also activates the GLP-1 receptor. No retatrutide label exists yet, so its own animal toxicology section has not been published in label form.

Who did the retatrutide trials exclude?

Lilly screened out people with the same risks the class warning names. The ClinicalTrials.gov records list this exclusion:

  • TRIUMPH-1 (obesity): "family or personal history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN-2)" (NCT05929066).
  • TRIUMPH-2 (obesity with type 2 diabetes): the same exclusion (NCT05929079).
  • TRIUMPH-3 (obesity with heart disease): the same exclusion (NCT05882045).
  • Cardiovascular and kidney outcomes trial: the same exclusion (NCT06383390).

The public summary for TRIUMPH-4, the knee osteoarthritis trial, lists a shorter set of exclusions and does not mention MTC (NCT05931367). Registry summaries are often abridged, so this does not mean high-risk people were enrolled.

The practical result: trial data cannot tell us how retatrutide behaves in people with MEN 2 or a family history of MTC. Those people were not studied.

Do human studies link GLP-1 drugs to thyroid cancer?

The large studies disagree. No study covers retatrutide itself. Here are two of the most cited, both in people with type 2 diabetes:

  • France, 2023. A nested case-control study in the national insurance database compared 2,562 people with thyroid cancer to 45,184 controls. Using GLP-1 drugs for 1 to 3 years was linked to higher risk of all thyroid cancer (hazard ratio 1.58) and MTC (hazard ratio 1.78) (Bezin et al., Diabetes Care 2023).
  • Denmark, Norway, and Sweden, 2024. A cohort compared 145,410 new GLP-1 drug users with 291,667 new DPP-4 inhibitor users. Thyroid cancer rates were 1.33 versus 1.46 per 10,000 person-years. The hazard ratio was 0.93, meaning no clear increase. For MTC, it was 1.19, with a wide 95% confidence interval of 0.37 to 3.86 (Pasternak et al., BMJ 2024).

The Scandinavian authors said their data fit with no more than a 31% relative increase. Mean follow-up was 3.9 years. MTC is so rare that both studies had few MTC cases, so those estimates are imprecise. People on newer drugs may also get more neck imaging, which can find small cancers that would otherwise go unseen. That is one reason the question stays open.

In absolute terms, thyroid cancer was uncommon in both Scandinavian groups: about 1 to 1.5 cases per 10,000 people per year.

What symptoms should you report?

The Zepbound label lists the thyroid tumor symptoms patients should know (DailyMed):

  1. A lump or swelling in the neck.
  2. Trouble swallowing (dysphagia).
  3. Shortness of breath (dyspnea).
  4. Hoarseness that does not go away.

The label also says a person with a thyroid nodule found on exam or neck imaging should be evaluated further. Very high calcitonin can point to MTC. Most people with MTC have calcitonin above 50 ng/L. For background on nodules and thyroid tests, see thyroid.md.

If you are in a retatrutide trial, report these symptoms to the study team. If you are buying unregulated "research" retatrutide, no one screened you for MEN 2 or a family history of MTC. Our page on gray-market retatrutide covers that risk.

The bottom line

Retatrutide has no boxed warning today because it has no FDA label. Lilly plans to file in early 2027. Approved drugs in the same class carry a boxed warning based on rat C-cell tumors, with an unknown human risk. Retatrutide's major Phase 3 trials excluded people with a personal or family history of MTC or MEN 2. Human studies of older GLP-1 drugs are mixed: one French study found higher risk, a larger Scandinavian study did not. If you have MEN 2 or a family history of MTC, raise it with your doctor before any drug in this class.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take retatrutide if a relative had medullary thyroid cancer?

Retatrutide is not approved, so there is no label to answer this yet. The TRIUMPH-1, TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3 trials excluded anyone with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2. Approved drugs in the class, such as Zepbound, list those conditions as contraindications.

Should I get a calcitonin test or thyroid ultrasound before a GLP-1 drug?

The Zepbound label says routine calcitonin testing or thyroid ultrasound is of uncertain value for early detection of medullary thyroid cancer. It notes low test specificity and a high background rate of thyroid disease. Your doctor may still test you if you have symptoms or a nodule.

Does the thyroid warning apply to common thyroid cancers like papillary cancer?

The boxed warning names thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma. Papillary and follicular cancers start in different thyroid cells. The French 2023 study did look at all thyroid cancers and reported a higher risk with 1 to 3 years of GLP-1 use, while the 2024 Scandinavian study did not.

Is hypothyroidism a reason to avoid retatrutide?

The class warning is about medullary thyroid cancer and MEN 2, not an underactive thyroid. The Zepbound label does not list hypothyroidism as a contraindication. Ask your doctor how weight loss might affect your thyroid hormone dose.

Will retatrutide definitely get the same boxed warning as Zepbound?

No one outside the FDA can say that yet. The FDA sets label wording during review, and Lilly has not yet filed. What is known is that approved GLP-1-based drugs, including Zepbound, carry the thyroid C-cell tumor warning.
